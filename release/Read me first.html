Cento

New chorales in the style of J. S. Bach, made from Bach's own.

Cento learns Bach's chorales beat by beat, then writes new ones by joining beats from different chorales, keeping the voice-leading and the shape of a real chorale. Then Magdalena learns the user's taste: what you like, as you rate what you hear. (She is named after Anna Magdalena Bach, singer and copyist of Bach's music; explain Magdalena, in the pop-up window, tells more.) (Cento is an independent project, inspired by David Cope's books; it isn't affiliated with him.)

Which one did you download?
  • Cento for Live: devices for Ableton Live 12 Suite (or Live 12 Standard with Max for Live), version 12.2.1 or later.
  • Cento for Max: a patch for Max 9, which is free to download and runs Cento without a paid licence.
Both have been tried on macOS only. Start with step 1, then follow your version's steps.

1. Put the Cento folder in Documents (both versions)

  1. Double-click the zip you downloaded, if it isn't unzipped already. A folder opens.
  2. In it, find the folder named Cento. Drag it onto Documents in the sidebar of the Finder window.

Cento looks for this folder every time it opens. It holds Cento's chorales, and later your settings and Magdalena's memory. Keep its name: Cento.

Updating from an earlier version? Keep the Cento folder you have: it holds your settings and Magdalena's memory.

2a. Cento for Live

  1. In the unzipped folder, open Cento Demo Project and double-click Cento Demo.als. Live opens a set with five tracks: Cento (the brain), and Soprano, Alto, Tenor and Bass.
  2. If macOS asks whether Live may access files in your Documents folder, click Allow.
  3. Press Live's Play button (or the space bar). The piece plays from the next bar, and the brain's piano roll follows it.
  4. For a new piece, change Seed on the brain.

In your own sets: the devices are in the Devices folder. Drag cento.brain.amxd onto a MIDI track, and cento.voice.amxd onto four MIDI tracks named Soprano, Alto, Tenor and Bass, each with an instrument. Tip: drag the Devices folder into Live's browser, under Places, to find them there.

2b. Cento for Max

  1. Max 9: if you don't have it, download it from cycling74.com/downloads and install it. You don't need to buy a licence to use Cento: without one, Max still opens and runs patches (a licence is only needed to save changes to them).
  2. In the unzipped folder, open Cento Patch and double-click cento.maxpat. Max opens it. (The other files in that folder are its parts: keep them together, and open only cento.maxpat.)
  3. If macOS asks whether Max may access files in your Documents folder, click Allow.
  4. Click the green play. If you hear nothing, set Output to AU DLS Synth 1 (the Mac's own instruments), and check the Mac's volume.
  5. For a new piece, change Seed.

You can keep the Cento Patch folder anywhere, for example in Documents beside the Cento folder.

3. Things to try

  • Hover over any control to see what it does. In Live, open the Info View first (the ? at the bottom left of Live's window).
  • like and dislike teach Magdalena your taste: later pieces lean toward what you liked. keep makes a piece part of Magdalena's notebook. The square ↗ button (top right) shows her taste in full.
  • corpora chooses which chorales Cento composes from. bach-figured-bass-3-4 alone gives pieces in 3/4.
  • export midi saves the piece as a MIDI file. In Live, write clips writes it as clips.

Where your files are

Everything Cento keeps is in Documents/Cento: the chorales (corpus), your settings and Magdalena's memory (the cento.*.json files). About this folder.txt in it says which is which. Pieces you export go where you choose.

More chorales

Cento comes with 131 Bach chorales. The free music21 toolkit has more (296 in 4/4 and 32 in 3/4), for your own use only. Getting them takes a few commands in Terminal; the steps are on Cento's page on GitHub.

Removing Cento

Delete the Cento Patch folder (or the devices and the demo project), and the Cento folder in Documents.

Known issues

  • This is a beta, tried on macOS only.
  • Cento for Live needs Live 12.2.1 or later (it includes Max 9). With an earlier Live, point Live at a Max 9 installation (Live's Settings, File & Folder, Max Application).
  • Cento for Max needs Max 9: it doesn't open in Max 8 or earlier.
  • Something wrong? Tell us at github.com/nnokes/cento/issues.
